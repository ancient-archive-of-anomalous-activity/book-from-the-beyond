Remember The Rise

When he first came down here, beneath the weight of the earth, this place was an arena. He was neither strong, nor skilled, nor did he know any strategy to speak of. But he was spiteful, stubborn, and persistent. No matter how many times he'd been beaten into the ground, no matter how many times he'd been crushed into the dust, no matter how many times his flesh failed him, no matter how many times he'd fallen down, over and over and over again, he'd stand back up, and yell at the hordes of challengers, beckoning them to attack him more, until he'd proven himself. Proven himself to whom? Even he did not know anymore, after enough time had passed. But he kept repeating himself, over and over again. He had forgotten everything; why he had come down there, how he had found a path to hell, and even his own name.

Somehow, after eons of failure, and suffering, and death, he had done the impossible, he had defeated a demon. And then he defeated the next. And the next. And then he defeated an angel. And then the next. And then the next. And from that point onward, he would win every fight he started. He had proven himself, to someone, somewhere. But he kept fighting, for a long, long time. He couldn't remember that he had intended to leave, eventually. So, he did not leave. For a very long time, he stayed. He continued to bite, and bleed, and scream, and scar himself, every day. Until so much time had passed, that the arena crumbled, and everyone abandoned it. Nobody comes here anymore, neither to spar nor to spectate. He had no opponents. No challengers. Nobody to tell him that he was too weak to fight. Nobody to spite. Nobody to smite. And nobody to prove himself to. Yet still he did not leave. He stayed until his own voice became foreign to him, and he spoke to himself aloud, as if to a stranger, and would respond to his own taunts towards himself. He stood alone in the arena.

Until he eventually asked himself why. What was he even doing there in the first place? Why did he stay? He didn't know, but he did stop fighting himself, and he lay still for a long time, until, for the first time in eons, he let himself sleep. And when he woke up, for the first time in eons, he felt hunger. But there was nothing to eat in the pit, it had nothing to offer but dust. So, he climbed out, and what he found waiting for him outside of the hole he had just climbed out of was a blackberry bush, littered with small bits and pieces of liver and bird meat. And he ate of it, both flesh and fruit. And he was finally sated, more than he ever had been whilst living off of the flesh and blood and bones of his enemies.

He had become feared among demonkind and angelkind alike. Yet he knew, deep in his heart, that the moment he returned to his own kind, he would become weak again, his strength only exists among the beasts of the beyond, so only the beasts of the beyond feared and respected him. He didn't want to be weak again. He remembered, finally, why he had descended into the pit in the first place. He couldn't tolerate being weak, but he was too weak to stop being weak, so he sought out a strong enemy who could kill him. He had come here to die. But the moment he came here, he forgot that, his impulses and instincts governed him too strongly to let him throw himself into death like that. So, he fought instead, in the name of his primal nature. The beyond bit him and he bit back harder. He bit off its flesh and ate it. He is one of us because he forced us to accept him. The one whose will to live is synonymous with spite. He didn't want to lose that, but he also couldn't stay. For staying in the pit was its own form of death, death in a different shroud. And he wanted to live, now that he had tasted what strength felt like. He returned to the land of the living, to try and become a fraction of what he was down here. As long as he's alive, the beasts of the beyond live in fear.
