Rememebr The Rise

When he first came down here, beneath the weight of the earth, this place was an arena. He was neither strong nor skilled nor did he know any strategy to speak of, but he was spiteful and stubborn and persistent. It was his persistence in the face of his powerlessness that proved to be a force so incontestable that no demon nor angel could ever beat him into submission. No matter how many times he'd been beaten into the ground, no matter how many times he'd been crushed into the dust, no matter how many times he'd drag himself across the floor of the arena, covered in his own blood and bile, no matter how many times his flesh failed him, over and over and over again, he'd stand up, or fall down trying to, and yell at the hordes of challengers, beckoning them to attack him more, until he'd proven himself. To whom? Even he did not know anymore, after enough time had passed. But he kept repeating the phrase. Over and over again. He had forgotten everything, why he came down there, how he had found a path to hell, and even his own name. Somehow, after eons of failure, and death, and suffering in his spite and stubbornness, he succeeded, for the first time, in defeating a demon. And then he defeated the next. And the next. And then he defeated an angel. And then the next. And then the next. From that point onward, he would win every fight he started. He stopped losing battles entirely. Never again would he be weak. Never again would he be forgotten. But he couldn't remember that he had intended to leave, eventually. So, he did not leave. For a very long time, he stayed. He continued to bite, and bleed, and scream, and scar himself, every day. Until so much time had passed, that the arena crumbled, and everyone had stopped visiting it, neither to spar nor to spectate. He had no opponents. No challengers. Nobody to tell him he was too weak to fight. Nobody to spite. Nobody to smite. And nobody to prove himself to. He was alone. Yet still he did not leave. He stayed until his own voice became foreign to him, and he spoke to himself aloud, as if to a stranger, and would respond to his own taunts towards himself. He eventually asked himself why. What was he even doing there in the first place? What was he even trying to prove? For what purpose, for what reason, and to whom? He hated how weak he was. He never wanted to survive. He had come there to die. The arena was now a pit. An empty pit of dust and bones and dried blood. He had gone down there to die. He hated his weakness. He wanted something big and strong to kill him. But it never came. He realized they could not kill him. No matter how weak he was. No matter how badly he wanted to die. There was something far too stubborn inside of him to let him simply fade away. He wanted to die but could not bring himself to execute himself. So he came down here. But nobody could beat him. The same will to live that kept him from killing himself had kept him alive despite all his wounds and infections and rot and starvation and sickness he had found and brought upon himself. There was nothing he could do. He was powerless against himself as we all inherently are. He came down there to die and found new life new strength. With strength he now wanted to live and fought to keep the strength he had finally gotten. But some part of him knew he had to lose it if he left. And he didn't want to lose it. And that is why he stayed for so long. He had to prove to himself that he would either die or get stronger. He had to prove himself wrong about his weakness. And when he finally did he still didn't want to leave. He had to stay. He had to stay strong. He had to stay here. Even after everyone had left. Even when he became alone in the arena. But he did leave. Eventually. He decided he wanted to live. After so much death had failed him. The only way he could exist was to keep living. But he needed his strength. It meant everything to him. So he made an oath to himself that he would become strong above as he had been below. And he left. And he felt hunger for the first time. And he stumbled upon a blackberry bush littered with small bits and pieces of liver. And he ate of it, both flesh and fruit. And he was finally sated more than he had been in tears on flesh and blood and bones of his enemies. He had become feared among demonkind and angelkind alike. And now he sought to become strong among humankind as well. And he returns to his birthplace among men. To challenge them instead. Razizrak, the one whose will to live is synonymous with spite. The one who bites out of spite with all his might. We speak of him amongst one another. His brothers will never know how feared he is among the beasts of the beyond. The beyond bit him and he bit back and he bit off its flesh and ate it. He is one of us because he forced us to accept him. He is a danger to things that lurk in the dark, posing a danger to his kind. We should all fear him. Razizrak, the ray of destruction that he is. His strength is not visible. But he will not give up until everybody sees it. He will be feared. He will be known. Razizrak the man who became a demon.
